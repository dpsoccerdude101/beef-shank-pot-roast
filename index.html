3. Method

  1. Salt the shank now, on all faces, and leave it on the counter 45 minutes, or overnight uncovered in the fridge. Trim the thick bands of silver skin but leave the bone in place.
  2. Heat the oven to 300°F.
  3. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high until it shimmers. Sear the shank on every reachable face, 5 to 6 minutes a side. It is an irregular shape, so work in stages and do not force it flat. Browning every face matters more here than on chuck. Shank has less fat, so the crust is carrying the flavor.
  4. Move the shank out. Soften the onion in the same pot, 4 minutes, scraping the dark bits up.
  5. Stir in the tomato paste and let it darken for 60 seconds against the hot pan.
  6. Pour in the wine and let it reduce by half, about 5 minutes. This is an extra step the original does not have, and it is worth it for shank.
  7. Add the broth, garlic, rosemary, thyme and bay leaf. Return the shank, bone side down if it has one. Liquid should come about halfway up the meat, not cover it.
  8. Bring to a bare simmer on the stovetop, then cover and braise 2½ hours.
  9. Add the potatoes, carrots and celery around the meat. Cover and go another 1½ to 2 hours, so 4 to 4½ hours total.
  10. Check at 3½ hours. Twist a fork in the thickest part: it should give almost no resistance, and the meat should be pulling back from the bone. Undercooked shank is the one failure that ruins the dish. If it fights the fork, give it 30 more minutes and check again.
  11. Lift the shank out, rest it 10 minutes, then pull or slice it. Discard the bay leaf. Skim the fat from the liquid, bring it to a simmer, and whisk in a cornstarch slurry a little at a time. Add more only if it stays thin.
  12. Serve in wide bowls with the sauce spooned over. Bread or boiled potatoes beat mashed here, the sauce is already rich.

5. Notes worth reading

  • The weight is mostly bone. 4.72 lb bone-in yields roughly 3.2 to 3.5 lb of meat, which is a normal batch. Do not scale the seasoning up for 4.72 or you will overseason the dish.
  • Why it takes longer than the original. Their 2 plus 2 hours is for chuck, which has intramuscular fat to keep it moist. Shank is collagen, not fat, so it needs past 4 hours to turn to gelatin. Pulling it at 3 hours gives you chewy meat, not tender meat.
  • Convection. Drop the dial to 275°F and expect roughly 20 minutes less. But the pot has a lid on, so the fan never touches the food: no extra browning and no drying out, which is the usual convection worry. Keep the pot clear of the back wall so it does not sit over the fan inlet.
  • Thicken less than the recipe says. Shank broth sets like jelly once chilled because of all the dissolved collagen. Start with 1 tbsp cornstarch, not 2.
  • Pot size. A 5.5 quart Dutch oven is tight once the vegetables go in. If it will not fit, braise the meat alone and roast the vegetables on a sheet pan for the last 40 minutes.
  • Do not add potatoes early. They turn to mush in 4 hours. Late addition is not optional.
  • Leftovers keep 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen. The flavor is better on day two.
